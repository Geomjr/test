# Part II Video Script: "Rate Shock" briefing to the CFO

About 2 minutes spoken (roughly 300 words). Lines in **[brackets]** tell you what to click or point at. Everything else is what you say out loud.

Before you hit record, open `rate_shock.html`, click **Reset** and scroll to the top.

---

### 0:00 – 0:15 · Opening

**[Tool open at the top, slider at 0]**

"Good morning. You asked how much trouble we're in if rates keep rising, and which bonds are the problem. I built a live tool to show you. It prices our four Treasuries off today's yield curve, and this slider lets me shift the whole curve up or down."

### 0:15 – 0:40 · The damage

"Right now the portfolio is worth three thousand five hundred twenty-six dollars."

**[Click +1%]**

"If rates rise one percent across the curve, we lose about three hundred fourteen dollars. That's almost nine percent of the portfolio."

**[Click +3%]**

"If they rise three percent, we lose about seven hundred ninety-five dollars. That's more than a fifth of our value, gone."

### 0:40 – 1:05 · Which bonds are the problem

**[Point at the "Which bonds get hurt" bar chart]**

"Look at where the losses come from. The two-year bond barely moves: it's down about five percent. The ten-year is down twenty. But the thirty-year bonds are down thirty-four and almost forty percent. The worst is the thirty-year bond with the two percent coupon."

**[Point at the lines chart, slide slowly from 0 up to +3%]**

"The steeper the line, the more rate risk. Our long bonds are where the danger is."

### 1:05 – 1:30 · Why: duration

**[Scroll down to the table, point at the Modified D\* column]**

"The number that explains this is duration. The two-year's modified duration is under two. The thirty-year two-percent bond's is eighteen and a half. Duration tells you roughly how much you lose, in percent, for every one percent rise in rates. It lines up with the slider almost exactly for a one-percent move. For bigger moves it slightly overstates the loss, because the price curve bends, and that bend is called convexity. The whole portfolio's duration is about nine point seven."

### 1:30 – 1:50 · Why long, low-coupon bonds hurt most, and SVB

"Long, low-coupon bonds pay most of their money back far in the future, and money that far out is hit hardest when rates go up. That's exactly what sank Silicon Valley Bank. It put short-term deposits into long-term bonds. When rates rose, those bonds lost value, depositors pulled their money, and the bank had to sell at a loss."

### 1:50 – 2:00 · Recommendation

**[Click Reset]**

"So, going into more rate hikes, I'd rather we hold shorter bonds. They lose far less, and they mature soon, so we can reinvest at the higher rates. I recommend we shorten our duration or hedge the long bonds."

---

**Recording tips**

- Talk to the camera as if the CFO is sitting across from you. Don't read word for word; glance at the script.
- If you run long, cut the convexity sentence in the duration section first.
- Share your screen, or put the tool on screen with your face in a corner. Either works.
