How the tool prices the bonds
  1. Par yields between the quoted maturities (1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 20, 30Y) are linearly interpolated to get a par yield for every year 1–30.
  2. Annual spot rates (discount factors) are bootstrapped from that par curve, since all four bonds pay annual coupons.
  3. Each bond is priced as the sum of its cash flows times the matching discount factor.
  4. A shock adds the same amount to every par yield, re-bootstraps the spot curve and reprices every bond.
  5. YTM is solved from each price. Macaulay D = Σ t·PV(CFt) / P, and modified D* = D / (1 + YTM).

Why the duration estimate drifts for big shocks: duration is a straight-line (first-order) approximation. The true price–yield curve bends (convexity), so for large rate rises the real loss is smaller than duration predicts, and for rate falls the real gain is larger.
